## Venkatesh 2003: context and aims
Q: What do Venkatesh et al. (2003) observe about IT acceptance research before this paper?
+ Many competing models with different sets of determinants forced researchers to "pick and choose" constructs
- Only one model was agreed on by all researchers and no debate remained
- No model explained more than 10% of the variance in users' intention to use technology
- The models were all identical, so there was no need to distinguish or compare them
E: The paper: "Researchers are confronted with a choice among a multitude of models and find that they must 'pick and choose' constructs across the models, or choose a 'favored model' and largely ignore the contributions from alternative models."

Q: According to the paper, what share of the variance in intention do technology acceptance models routinely explain?
+ Typically over 40%
- Typically under 5%
- Typically exactly 100%
- Typically about 10%
E: The paper: "Research in this area has resulted in several theoretical models... that routinely explain over 40 percent of the variance in individual intention to use technology".

Q: Which estimate about organizations' IT investment opens the paper?
+ Since the 1980s, about 50% of organizations' new capital investment has been in information technology
- Since the 1980s, about 5% of organizations' new capital investment has been in information technology
- Since the 1960s, about 100% of organizations' new capital investment has been in information technology
- Since the 1990s, about 25% of organizations' new capital investment has been in information technology
E: The paper: "Some estimates indicate that, since the 1980s, about 50 percent of all new capital investment in organizations has been in information technology (Westland and Clark 2000)."

Q: What are the paper's four objectives?
+ Review eight models; compare them empirically; formulate a unified model; validate the unified model empirically
- Collect data; clean data; analyze data; publish data for the community
- Design software; program software; test software; deploy software
- Build scales; translate scales; check scales; sell rights to scales
E: The paper lists: "(1) To review the extant user acceptance models... (2) To empirically compare the eight models... (3) To formulate the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)... (4) To empirically validate UTAUT".

Q: Which dependent variable does the paper focus on in the models it considers?
+ Intention and/or usage
- Organizational satisfaction
- Implementation success at the organizational level
- Task-technology fit
E: The paper: "the theoretical models to be included in the present review, comparison, and synthesis employ intention and/or usage as the key dependent variable. The goal here is to understand usage as the dependent variable."

Q: Which eight models does the paper consider?
+ TRA, TAM, the motivational model, TPB, the combined TAM-TPB model, the model of PC utilization, innovation diffusion theory and social cognitive theory
- TRA, TAM, UTAUT, TPB, game theory, behavioral learning theory, evolutionary theory and chaos theory
- TAM, TAM2, TAM3, TAM4, TAM5, TAM6, TAM7 and TAM8 as numbered successive versions
- Conflict theory, structural theory, systems theory, exchange theory, control theory, role theory, group theory and network theory
E: The abstract: "The eight models reviewed are the theory of reasoned action, the technology acceptance model, the motivational model, the theory of planned behavior, a model combining the technology acceptance model and the theory of planned behavior, the model of PC utilization, the innovation diffusion theory, and the social cognitive theory."

Q: How many determinants do the eight models hypothesize and how many constructs in total?
+ Between two and seven determinants; 32 constructs in total
- Between one and three determinants; 8 constructs in total
- Between ten and fifteen determinants; 100 constructs in total
- Exactly four determinants per model; 32 constructs in total
E: The paper: "The models hypothesize between two and seven determinants of acceptance, for a total of 32 constructs across the eight models."

Q: Which four moderators have been found significant alongside the earlier models?
+ Experience, voluntariness, gender and age
- Education, income, occupation and hometown
- Race, religion, political views and language
- Weight, height, eyesight and hearing
E: The paper: "Table 2 identifies four key moderating variables (experience, voluntariness, gender, and age) that have been found to be significant in conjunction with these models."

Q: How many empirical comparisons of two or more of the models had been published in major IS journals before this paper?
+ Four studies
- Forty studies
- None
- A single study
E: The paper: "there have only been four studies reporting empirically-based comparisons of two or more of the eight models published in the major information systems journals."

Q: What limit of earlier comparison studies concerned participants, and how does this paper address it?
+ Three of the four used students; this paper uses data from employees in organizations
- All four used employees; this paper uses data from university students
- Three of the four used children; this paper uses data from retirees
- None had participants; this paper uses computer-simulated data
E: The paper: "the participants in three of the four model comparison studies have been students—only Plouffe et al. (2001) conducted their research in a nonacademic setting. This research is conducted using data collected from employees in organizations."

Q: What limit of earlier studies concerned the timing of measurement?
+ Most measured well after the acceptance decision, when behavior had become routine, so reactions were retrospective
- Most measured before users knew anything about the technology, so there were no real reactions
- Most measured on the very day users received the technology, before any experience
- Most measured daily for many years, so there was too much data to analyze
E: The paper: "most of the tests of the eight models were conducted well after the participants' acceptance or rejection decision rather than during the active adoption decision-making process. Because behavior has become routinized, individual reactions reported in those studies are retrospective".

Q: What limit of earlier studies concerned voluntary versus mandatory contexts?
+ Most tests, and all four comparisons, were conducted in voluntary contexts
- Most tests were in mandatory contexts and nobody considered voluntary ones
- No study distinguished voluntary from mandatory because the two are identical
- Most tests were in military contexts with users who had no choice
E: The paper: "Most of the model tests and all four model comparisons were conducted in voluntary usage contexts. Therefore, one must use caution when generalizing those results to mandatory settings".

## Venkatesh 2003: empirical comparison of the eight models
Q: What is the design of the study comparing the eight models?
+ A longitudinal field study at four organizations with three measurements and six months of tracked usage
- A one-off lab experiment with first-year students and no tracking of use
- A single online cross-sectional survey of thousands of people across all countries
- In-depth interviews with three users at one company with no quantitative scales
E: The paper: "Longitudinal field studies were conducted at four organizations among individuals being introduced to a new technology in the workplace... Actual usage behavior was measured over the six-month post-training period."

Q: When were the three questionnaire measurements taken?
+ Post-training (T1), one month after implementation (T2) and three months after implementation (T3)
- Before training, right after training and one year after training
- One week, one month and one year after the company bought the technology
- Every day during the first week after the technology was deployed
E: The paper: "A pretested questionnaire containing items measuring constructs from all eight models was administered at three different points in time: post-training (T1), one month after implementation (T2), and three months after implementation (T3)."

Q: How was actual usage behavior measured?
+ As duration of use from system logs; the system logged off inactive users after 5 to 15 minutes
- By self-reported frequency on a seven-point scale at each measurement
- By managers rating each employee's use at the end of the month
- By counting technical support requests during the six months
E: The paper: "Actual usage behavior was measured as duration of use via system logs... the system automatically logged off inactive users after a period of 5 to 15 minutes, eliminating most idle time from the usage logs."

Q: How was behavioral intention measured?
+ A three-item scale adapted from Davis et al. (1989), on seven-point scales
- A single yes/no question about using the system
- A ten-item scale adapted from Rogers (1995) on five-point scales
- From system logs instead of the questionnaire at all three times
E: The paper: "Behavioral intention to use the system was measured using a three-item scale adapted from Davis et al. (1989)... Seven-point scales were used for all of the aforementioned constructs' measurement".

Q: Why did the verb tense in the scales differ across measurement times?
+ Future tense at T1 and present tense at T2 and T3, reflecting the timing of measurement
- Past tense at every time so users could recall their experience
- Future tense at T3 because users had no real experience after three months
- There was no difference; all scales used one tense to avoid measurement bias
E: The paper: "The tense of the verbs in the various scales reflected the timing of measurement: future tense was employed at T1, present tense was employed at T2 and T3 (see Karahanna et al. 1999)."

Q: How did studies 1 and 2 differ in setting?
+ Study 1 was voluntary (M ≈ 6.5 of 7); study 2 was mandatory (M ≈ 1.5)
- Study 1 was mandatory (M ≈ 1.5); study 2 was voluntary (M ≈ 6.5)
- Both were voluntary with M ≈ 6.5 but differed in participants' occupations
- Both were mandatory with M ≈ 1.5 but differed in company size
E: The paper: "The perceptions of voluntariness were very high in studies 1a and 1b (1a: M = 6.50, SD = 0.22; 1b: M = 6.51, SD = 0.20) and very low in studies 2a and 2b (1a: M = 1.50...; 1b: M = 1.49...)."

Q: Why was the data split into two sets (studies 1a and 1b versus 2a and 2b)?
+ Because perceived voluntariness was bimodal (very high or very low), consistent with Venkatesh and Davis (2000)
- Because the first two organizations were in Asia and the last two in Europe, so cultures differed
- Because the first two used desktop computers and the last two used laptops
- Because the statistical software could process at most 120 participants per run
E: The paper: "Given this bi-modal distribution in the data (voluntary vs. mandatory), we created two data sets: (1) studies 1a and 1b, and (2) studies 2a and 2b."

Q: What are the sample sizes of the two data sets?
+ 119 in the voluntary setting and 96 in the mandatory setting (215 in total)
- 215 in the voluntary setting and 96 in the mandatory setting (311 in total)
- 50 in each setting (100 in total)
- 645 in the voluntary setting and 399 in the mandatory setting
E: Table 5: Study 1 (N = 119); Table 6: Study 2 (N = 96); pooled: "a sample of 645 (215 x 3)".

Q: What were the industry and system of study 2a (mandatory)?
+ Banking; a portfolio analyzer analysts were required to use to evaluate accounts
- Entertainment; an online meeting manager used voluntarily instead of in-person meetings
- Telecommunications; a database used voluntarily to look up industry standards for products
- Retail electronics; an application required for documenting and managing service contracts
E: Table 4: "2a Banking, Business Account Management, 58: Portfolio analyzer that analysts were required to use in evaluating existing and potential accounts".

Q: What main statistical technique was used to test the models?
+ PLS (partial least squares) with 500 bootstrap samples
- One-way ANOVA with simple t-tests
- Logistic regression with Cox time-to-event models
- Multilevel linear regression with 50 bootstrap samples
E: The paper: "Partial least squares (PLS Graph...) was used to examine the reliability and validity of the measures... we employed a bootstrapping method (500 times)".

Q: What were the results on loadings and reliabilities in the comparison?
+ Most loadings were .70 or higher and all internal consistency reliabilities exceeded .70
- Most loadings were below .30 and all internal consistency reliabilities were below .50
- Loadings and reliabilities were not reported because the scales were already validated
- All loadings and reliabilities equaled exactly 1.00 due to data processing
E: The paper: "The loading pattern was found to be acceptable with most loadings being .70 or higher. All internal consistency reliabilities were greater than .70."

Q: What was the first finding when comparing the eight models?
+ All eight models explained individual acceptance, with variance in intention explained ranging from 17% to 42%
- Only one of the eight models explained acceptance and the others failed
- All eight explained over 90% of the variance in intention in every setting
- None explained more than 1% of the variance in participants' intention
E: The paper: "First, all eight models explained individual acceptance, with variance in intention explained ranging from 17 percent to 42 percent."

Q: What was the key difference between voluntary and mandatory studies in the comparison?
+ Social-influence constructs were significant in mandatory settings but not in voluntary settings
- Social-influence constructs were significant in voluntary settings but not in mandatory settings
- All constructs were significant in both settings with no difference between them
- No construct was significant in mandatory settings, so the models work only in voluntary ones
E: The paper: "in mandatory settings (study 2), constructs related to social influence were significant whereas in the voluntary settings (study 1), they were not significant."

Q: How did the determinants of intention change over time?
+ Some determinants went from significant to nonsignificant as experience increased
- All determinants started nonsignificant and became significant as experience increased
- All determinants kept the same significance at all three times with no change over time
- All determinants lost significance right after the first time point and never regained it
E: The paper: "the determinants of intention varied over time, with some determinants going from significant to nonsignificant with increasing experience."

Q: How were the moderators coded when pooling (experience, voluntariness, gender, age)?
+ Voluntariness a dummy variable; gender a 0/1 dummy; age continuous; experience ordinal 0, 1, 2 for T1, T2, T3
- All four continuous variables standardized to mean zero and standard deviation one
- All four 0/1 dummies with no information on degree or order
- Only age was included; the other three were dropped for lack of data
E: The paper: "Voluntariness was a dummy variable... Gender was coded as a 0/1 dummy variable... age was coded as a continuous variable... Experience was operationalized via a dummy variable that took ordinal values of 0, 1, or 2 to capture increasing levels of user experience".

Q: What was the sample size after pooling the three time points, and what limitation of pooling was noted?
+ 645 (215 × 3); repeated measures on the same people mean measurement errors may be correlated; Chow tests confirmed the pattern
- 215 (215 × 1); repeated measures do not exist, so there was no limitation to note
- 1290 (215 × 6); repeated measures cancel errors so no further checking was needed
- 100 (subsample); repeated measures raise power so they are an advantage, not a limitation
E: The paper: "Pooling the data across the three points of measurement resulted in a sample of 645 (215 x 3)... One of the limitations of pooling is that there are repeated measures from the same individuals, resulting in measurement errors that are potentially correlated across time. However, cross-sectional analysis using Chow's (1960) test..."

Q: What does the paper report about TAM2 and TAM with gender when moderators were added?
+ Explained variance rose to 53% (TAM2) and 52% (TAM including gender), versus about 35% in cross-sectional tests without moderators
- Explained variance fell to 5% for both, versus about 35% in cross-sectional tests
- Explained variance stayed at about 35% with or without moderators
- Explained variance rose to 99% for both because the model had many moderators
E: The paper: "the variance explained by TAM2 increased to 53 percent and TAM including gender increased to 52 percent when compared to approximately 35 percent in cross-sectional tests of TAM (without moderators)."

Q: Why do the authors caution about comparing variance across models with moderators?
+ Because TAM-family models have more moderators in the literature, which may have unintentionally biased results in their favor
- Because TAM-family models are always worse, so extra moderators were added for fairness
- Because the other models had been validated on three million people and can be compared directly
- Because moderators apply only to TAM and other models do not allow moderators
E: The paper: "in the case of TAM and its variations, the extensive prior empirical work has suggested a larger number of moderators when compared to moderators suggested for other models. This in turn may have unintentionally biased the results and contributed to the high variance explained in TAM-related models when compared to the other models."

Q: Besides intention, which factor became a direct determinant of use at T3 in the comparison?
+ Perceived behavioral control, showing continued use can be directly hindered or fostered by resources and opportunities
- Subjective norm, showing continued use is decided by coworkers' pressure
- Attitude, showing continued use is decided by how much people enjoy the technology
- Anxiety, showing continued use is decided by fear of losing data through wrong actions
E: The paper: "perceived behavioral control became a significant direct determinant of use over and above intention with increasing experience (at T3) indicating that continued use could be directly hindered or fostered by resources and opportunities."

Q: How strongly did intention predict use in the comparison (Table 8)?
+ β about .58 to .61, significant at all times and in both settings
- β about .08 to .11, mostly nonsignificant across times and settings
- β about .98 to 1.00, nearly identical to actual use
- Negative β, showing high intention goes with lower use at all times
E: Table 8: BI → Use: .61***, .60***, .58*** (voluntary) and .58***, .61***, .56*** (mandatory).

Q: Which three consistent findings from the model comparisons led to UTAUT?
+ Every model has a strongest, always-significant construct; several constructs lose significance; voluntary versus mandatory context affects the social constructs
- No model has a significant construct; all constructs gain significance; context has no effect
- Every model has exactly one significant construct; none changes; context affects every construct
- Only TAM has significant constructs; other models fail; context affects only attitude
E: The paper: "First, for every model, there was at least one construct that was significant in all time periods and that construct also had the strongest influence... Second, several other constructs were initially significant, but then became nonsignificant over time... Finally, the voluntary vs. mandatory context did have an influence on the significance of constructs related to social influence".

Q: What were the strongest, always-significant constructs of TAM and MPCU, respectively?
+ Perceived usefulness (TAM) and job-fit (MPCU)
- Perceived ease of use (TAM) and complexity (MPCU)
- Subjective norm (TAM) and social factors (MPCU)
- Attitude (TAM) and facilitating conditions (MPCU)
E: The paper: "perceived usefulness in TAM/TAM2 and C-TAM-TPB, extrinsic motivation in MM, job-fit in MPCU, relative advantage in IDT, and outcome expectations in SCT".

Q: Which social constructs were significant only in mandatory settings?
+ Subjective norm (TPB, C-TAM-TPB, TAM2), social factors (MPCU) and image (IDT)
- Perceived behavioral control (TPB), facilitating conditions (MPCU) and compatibility (IDT)
- Perceived usefulness (TAM), job-fit (MPCU) and relative advantage (IDT)
- Self-efficacy (SCT), anxiety (SCT) and affect (SCT)
E: The paper: "subjective norm (TPB/DTPB, C-TAM-TPB and TAM2), social factors (MPCU), and image (IDT) were only significant in mandatory implementations."

Q: Which constructs were initially significant and then became nonsignificant over time?
+ Perceived behavioral control, perceived ease of use, complexity, ease of use (IDT), self-efficacy and anxiety
- Perceived usefulness, job-fit, relative advantage, extrinsic motivation and outcome expectations
- Subjective norm, social factors, image, attitude and intrinsic motivation
- None; every construct was equally significant at all three times
E: The paper: "several other constructs were initially significant, but then became nonsignificant over time, including perceived behavioral control in TPB/DTPB and C-TAM-TPB, perceived ease of use in TAM/TAM2, complexity in MPCU, ease of use in IDT, and self-efficacy and anxiety in SCT."

## Venkatesh 2003: UTAUT
Q: How many core determinants and how many moderators does UTAUT have?
+ Four core constructs (three for intention, plus facilitating conditions for use, with intention also for use) and up to four moderators
- Two core constructs and eight moderators
- Thirty-two core constructs and no moderators
- One core construct and one moderator
E: The abstract: "UTAUT... with four core determinants of intention and usage, and up to four moderators of key relationships."

Q: What are UTAUT's four core constructs?
+ Performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions
- Attitude, subjective norm, behavioral control and behavioral intention
- Usefulness, ease of use, self-efficacy and anxiety
- Relative advantage, compatibility, complexity and observability
E: The paper: "we theorize that four constructs will play a significant role as direct determinants of user acceptance and usage behavior: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions."

Q: Which three factors directly determine intention to use in UTAUT?
+ Performance expectancy, effort expectancy and social influence
- Performance expectancy, facilitating conditions and attitude
- Social influence, facilitating conditions and anxiety
- Effort expectancy, self-efficacy and attitude
E: The paper: "UTAUT, which posits three direct determinants of intention to use (performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and social influence) and two direct determinants of usage behavior (intention and facilitating conditions)."

Q: Which two factors directly determine usage behavior?
+ Behavioral intention and facilitating conditions
- Performance expectancy and effort expectancy
- Social influence and attitude
- Self-efficacy and anxiety
E: The paper: "two direct determinants of usage behavior (intention and facilitating conditions)".

Q: Which constructs are theorized NOT to be direct determinants of intention?
+ Attitude toward using technology, self-efficacy and anxiety
- Performance expectancy, effort expectancy and social influence
- Behavioral intention, use and facilitating conditions
- Gender, age and experience
E: The paper: "attitude toward using technology, self-efficacy, and anxiety are theorized not to be direct determinants of intention."

Q: How is performance expectancy defined?
+ The degree to which an individual believes that using the system will help him or her attain gains in job performance
- The degree of ease associated with the use of the system
- The degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe he or she should use the new system
- The degree to which an individual believes that an organizational and technical infrastructure exists to support use
E: The paper: "Performance expectancy is defined as the degree to which an individual believes that using the system will help him or her to attain gains in job performance."

Q: How is effort expectancy defined?
+ The degree of ease associated with the use of the system
- The degree to which an individual believes that using the system will help attain gains in job performance
- The degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe he or she should use the new system
- The degree to which an individual has positive or negative feelings about using the system
E: The paper: "Effort expectancy is defined as the degree of ease associated with the use of the system."

Q: How is social influence defined?
+ The degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe he or she should use the new system
- The degree of ease associated with the use of the system in daily work
- The degree to which an individual believes that using the system will help attain gains in job performance
- The degree to which an individual perceives that the organization's technical infrastructure is enough to support use
E: The paper: "Social influence is defined as the degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe he or she should use the new system."

Q: How are facilitating conditions defined?
+ The degree to which an individual believes that an organizational and technical infrastructure exists to support use of the system
- The degree of ease associated with the use of the system in daily work
- The degree to which an individual perceives that important others believe he or she should use the new system
- The degree to which an individual believes that using the system will enhance his or her job performance
E: The paper: "Facilitating conditions are defined as the degree to which an individual believes that an organizational and technical infrastructure exists to support use of the system."

Q: Which five constructs does performance expectancy merge?
+ Perceived usefulness (TAM, C-TAM-TPB), extrinsic motivation (MM), job-fit (MPCU), relative advantage (IDT), outcome expectations (SCT)
- Subjective norm (TRA), social factors (MPCU), image (IDT), attitude (TPB), anxiety (SCT)
- Perceived ease of use (TAM), complexity (MPCU), ease of use (IDT), self-efficacy (SCT), affect (SCT)
- Behavioral control (TPB), facilitating conditions (MPCU), compatibility (IDT), trialability (IDT), visibility (IDT)
E: The paper: "The five constructs from the different models that pertain to performance expectancy are perceived usefulness (TAM/TAM2 and C-TAM-TPB), extrinsic motivation (MM), job-fit (MPCU), relative advantage (IDT), and outcome expectations (SCT)."

Q: Which three constructs does effort expectancy merge?
+ Perceived ease of use (TAM/TAM2), complexity (MPCU) and ease of use (IDT)
- Perceived usefulness (TAM), job-fit (MPCU) and relative advantage (IDT)
- Subjective norm (TRA), social factors (MPCU) and image (IDT)
- Behavioral control (TPB), facilitating conditions (MPCU) and compatibility (IDT)
E: The paper: "Three constructs from the existing models capture the concept of effort expectancy: perceived ease of use (TAM/TAM2), complexity (MPCU), and ease of use (IDT)."

Q: Which three constructs does social influence merge?
+ Subjective norm (TRA, TAM2, TPB/DTPB, C-TAM-TPB), social factors (MPCU) and image (IDT)
- Perceived usefulness (TAM), job-fit (MPCU) and relative advantage (IDT)
- Perceived ease of use (TAM), complexity (MPCU) and ease of use (IDT)
- Attitude (TRA), affect toward use (MPCU) and intrinsic motivation (MM)
E: The paper: "Table 11 presents the three constructs related to social influence: subjective norm (TRA, TAM2, TPB/DTPB, and C-TAM-TPB), social factors (MPCU), and image (IDT)."

Q: Which three constructs does facilitating conditions merge?
+ Perceived behavioral control (TPB/DTPB, C-TAM-TPB), facilitating conditions (MPCU) and compatibility (IDT)
- Subjective norm (TRA), social factors (MPCU) and image (IDT)
- Perceived usefulness (TAM), job-fit (MPCU) and relative advantage (IDT)
- Perceived ease of use (TAM), complexity (MPCU) and ease of use (IDT)
E: The paper: "This definition captures concepts embodied by three different constructs: perceived behavioral control (TPB/DTPB, C-TAM-TPB), facilitating conditions (MPCU), and compatibility (IDT)."

- The influence of performance expectancy on intention is moderated by gender and age: stronger for older women
- The influence of performance expectancy on intention is not moderated by any variable
- The influence of performance expectancy on intention is moderated only by experience and voluntariness
E: The paper: "H1: The influence of performance expectancy on behavioral intention will be moderated by gender and age, such that the effect will be stronger for men and particularly for younger men."

- The influence on use is stronger for younger workers at early stages
- The influence on use is not moderated by any variable

Q: What does hypothesis H5 (a, b, c) state?
+ Computer self-efficacy, computer anxiety and attitude have no significant direct influence on intention
- Self-efficacy, anxiety and attitude all have the strongest influence on intention
- Only attitude has no influence, while self-efficacy and anxiety have strong influence
- Only anxiety has no influence, while self-efficacy and attitude have strong influence
E: The paper: "H5a: Computer self-efficacy will not have a significant influence on behavioral intention. H5b: Computer anxiety will not have a significant influence on behavioral intention. H5c: Attitude toward using technology will not have a significant influence on behavioral intention."

Q: What does hypothesis H6 state?
+ Behavioral intention has a significant positive influence on usage
- Behavioral intention has no significant influence on usage
- Behavioral intention influences usage only for younger men
- Behavioral intention has a negative influence on usage
E: The paper: "H6: Behavioral intention will have a significant positive influence on usage."

Q: Why do the authors expect the effect of performance expectancy to be moderated by gender and age?
+ Men tend to be task-oriented so performance is more salient; younger workers value extrinsic rewards more; per gender role theory
- Because men are always smarter than women and younger people always better at technology
- Because labor law requires men and younger people to use technology more than others
- Because companies deployed new technology only to men and younger people during the study
E: The paper: "men tend to be highly task-oriented and, therefore, performance expectancies, which focus on task accomplishment, are likely to be especially salient to men. Gender schema theory suggests that such differences stem from gender roles and socialization processes... younger workers may place more importance on extrinsic rewards."

Q: What do the individual-model results show for effort expectancy over time?
+ Significant in both settings but only in the early period (T1), then nonsignificant with extended use
- Significant at all times and in both settings, stronger with more experience
- Not significant at any time in either setting
- Significant only at T3 after users have used the technology for a long time
E: The paper: "The effort expectancy construct within each model (Table 10) is significant in both voluntary and mandatory usage contexts; however, each one is significant only during the first time period (post-training, T1), becoming nonsignificant over periods of extended and sustained usage".

Q: How does the paper explain effort-related constructs mattering only early?
+ They are salient when the behavior is new and process issues are hurdles, then overshadowed by instrumentality concerns
- They matter only for young users so they lose significance as users grow older over time
- They matter only for forced users and vanish once people are allowed to choose freely
- They were dropped from the questionnaire at later times so no data exist on their significance
E: The paper: "Effort-oriented constructs are expected to be more salient in the early stages of a new behavior, when process issues represent hurdles to be overcome, and later become overshadowed by instrumentality concerns".

Q: What three mechanisms does social influence use to affect behavior, per the paper?
+ Compliance, internalization and identification
- Imitation, rivalry and competition
- Reward, punishment and neglect
- Advertising, promotion and discounts
E: The paper: "Social influence has an impact on individual behavior through three mechanisms: compliance, internalization, and identification (see Venkatesh and Davis 2000; Warshaw 1980)."

Q: Why does social influence act directly on intention in mandatory settings?
+ Through compliance: users simply change intention in response to social pressure, especially when others can reward or punish
- Through internalization: users deeply change their own belief system
- Because users always enjoy following superiors' orders in every case
- Because mandatory systems always have higher performance expectancy than voluntary ones
E: The paper: "the compliance mechanism causes an individual to simply alter his or her intention in response to the social pressure—i.e., the individual intends to comply with the social influence. Prior research suggests that individuals are more likely to comply with others' expectations when those referent others have the ability to reward the desired behavior or punish nonbehavior".

Q: By what mechanism does social influence operate in voluntary settings, per Venkatesh and Davis (2000)?
+ By influencing perceptions about the technology, through internalization and identification
- Through compliance, as users change intention to avoid punishment
- Through direct administrative orders from a government regulator
- It does not operate through any mechanism because social influence does not exist in voluntary settings
E: The paper: "social influence in voluntary contexts operates by influencing perceptions about the technology—the mechanisms at play here are internalization and identification."

Q: What does the paper say about experience and social influence in mandatory settings?
+ It matters mainly in the early stages; its role erodes with time and is nonsignificant with sustained use (T3)
- It matters most in the final stage when users have much experience
- It does not change with experience and is equally strong at every time
- It disappears after the first day and does not reappear at later times
E: The paper: "In mandatory settings, social influence appears to be important only in the early stages of individual experience with the technology, with its role eroding over time and eventually becoming nonsignificant with sustained usage (T3)".

Q: Why do facilitating conditions not predict intention when performance and effort expectancy are present?
+ Support-infrastructure issues are largely captured by effort expectancy; Venkatesh (2000) found full mediation by effort expectancy
- Because facilitating conditions never relate to technology use in any circumstance
- Because facilitating conditions can be measured only in young male users in voluntary settings
- Because organizations never provide support infrastructure for users of new technology
E: The paper: "issues related to the support infrastructure—a core concept within the facilitating conditions construct—are largely captured within the effort expectancy construct which taps the ease with which that tool can be applied... Venkatesh (2000) found support for full mediation of the effect of facilitating conditions on intention by effort expectancy."

Q: When can facilitating conditions predict intention (as in TPB/DTPB)?
+ When effort expectancy is not in the model
- When performance expectancy is removed from the model
- When users have never used any technology before
- When the organization mandates that everyone use the new technology
E: The paper: "if effort expectancy is not present in the model (as is the case with TPB/DTPB), then one would expect facilitating conditions to become predictive of intention."

Q: Why do the authors regard the attitude-intention relationship as "spurious"?
+ Attitude is significant only when performance and effort expectancy are absent, so the relation arises from omitted key predictors
- Because attitude is always the only determinant of intention in every model and setting
- Because attitude cannot be measured with any scale, so all attitude results are made up
- Because attitude is significant only for young men, so it cannot be generalized to all users
E: The paper: "the attitudinal constructs are significant only when specific cognitions—in this case, constructs related to performance and effort expectancies—are not included in the model... we consider any observed relationship between attitude and intention to be spurious and resulting from the omission of the other key predictors".

Q: Which four constructs in the original models align with "attitude" (general affective reaction to using)?
+ Attitude toward behavior, intrinsic motivation, affect toward use (MPCU) and affect (SCT)
- Usefulness, ease of use, subjective norm and perceived behavioral control
- Relative advantage, compatibility, visibility and trialability
- Performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions
E: The paper: "Four constructs from the existing models align closely with this definition: attitude toward behavior (TRA, TPB/DTPB, C-TAM-TPB), intrinsic motivation (MM), affect toward use (MPCU), and affect (SCT)."

Q: How are self-efficacy and anxiety treated in UTAUT?
+ Distinct from effort expectancy but acting indirectly through it, so no direct effect on intention beyond effort expectancy
- Identical to effort expectancy, so merged into a single variable in the model
- The strongest direct determinants of intention in every user group
- Unrelated to technology acceptance, so dropped from the paper entirely
E: The paper: "Previous research (Venkatesh 2000) has shown self-efficacy and anxiety to be conceptually and empirically distinct from effort expectancy... modeled as indirect determinants of intention fully mediated by perceived ease of use... to be distinct from effort expectancy and to have no direct effect on intention above and beyond effort expectancy."

## Venkatesh 2003: testing UTAUT
Q: Which data did the initial test of UTAUT use?
+ T1 data pooled across four organizations (N = 215) for the measurement model, and three time points pooled (N = 645) for pooled analysis
- Data from two entirely new organizations (N = 133) collected after the paper was accepted
- Simulated data with 10,000 virtual users under different conditions
- Data from a single organization (N = 54) in a voluntary setting
E: The paper: "Using the post-training data (T1) pooled across studies (N = 215), a measurement model of the seven direct determinants of intention... was estimated... Pooling the data across the three points of measurement resulted in a sample of 645 (215 x 3)."

- Because the remaining items were all wrong and proven unrelated to any construct
- Because the journal requires only four items per construct in every published paper
- Because the remaining items were left blank by participants so no data existed
E: The paper: "Although the structural model was tested on all the items, the sample size poses a limitation here because of the number of latent variables and associated items. Therefore, we reanalyzed the data using only four of the highest loading items".

Q: How did performance expectancy act on intention (H1)?
+ Via a three-way interaction: moderated by gender and age, more salient to younger workers, particularly men
- As a simple main effect not moderated by gender or age
- Only for older women and for no other group
- Not significant at any time and for any user group
E: The paper: "the effect of performance expectancy was in the form of a three-way interaction—the effect was moderated by gender and age such that it was more salient to younger workers, particularly men, thus supporting H1."

Q: Why is the main effect of performance expectancy "not interpretable" in the analysis with interactions?
+ Because higher-order interaction terms are present, so main effects cannot be interpreted independently
- Because the main effect equals exactly zero at every time and there are no data to interpret
- Because the software did not print main effects so the authors could not see them
- Because the main effect was so large it was treated as measurement error and dropped
E: The paper: "a direct effect for performance expectancy on intention was observed; however, these main effects are not interpretable due to the presence of interaction terms (e.g., Aiken and West 1991)."

Q: What was the result for effort expectancy (H2)?
+ A three-way interaction: more salient to women, especially older women, and more significant with limited exposure (decreasing with experience)
- No interaction: equal effect for all genders, ages and experience levels
- Salient only for young men with much experience and not significant for other groups
- Not significant at any time and for any user group
E: The paper: "The effect of effort expectancy was via a three-way interaction—the effect was moderated by gender and age (more salient to women and more so to older women). Based on Chow's test of beta differences (p < .05), effort expectancy was more significant with limited exposure to the technology (effect decreasing with experience), thus supporting H2."

Q: What was the result for social influence (H3)?
+ A four-way interaction: more important in mandatory contexts, among women, especially older women, and in the early stages of experience
- A simple main effect: equally strong in all settings, genders, ages and experience levels
- Not significant under any condition and for any user group
- Significant only in voluntary settings, for young men and at later stages of experience
E: The paper: "The effect of social influence was via a four-way interaction—with its role being more important in the context of mandatory use, more so among women, and even more so among older women. The Chow's test of beta differences (p < .05) indicated that social influence was even more significant in the early stages of individual experience with the technology, thus supporting H3."

Q: What were the results for facilitating conditions on intention (H4a) and on use (H4b)?
+ Nonsignificant for intention; significant for use, moderated by age (stronger for older workers) and stronger with experience
- Significant for intention; nonsignificant for use in every group
- Significant for both intention and use, the same in every user group
- Nonsignificant for both intention and use in every user group
E: The paper: "Facilitating conditions was nonsignificant as a determinant of intention, thus supporting H4a... In predicting usage behavior, both behavioral intention (H6) and facilitating conditions were significant, with the latter's effect being moderated by age (the effect being more important to older workers); and... the effect was stronger with increasing experience with the technology (H4b)."

Q: What were the results for self-efficacy, anxiety and attitude (H5)?
+ No direct effect on intention, supporting H5a, H5b and H5c
- The strongest direct effect on intention, rejecting H5a, H5b and H5c
- Only attitude had an effect; self-efficacy and anxiety did not
- Only anxiety had an effect; self-efficacy and attitude did not
E: The paper: "As expected self-efficacy, anxiety, and attitude did not have any direct effect on intention, thus supporting H5a, H5b, and H5c."

Q: What did the power analysis for the "no effect" hypotheses show?
+ The likelihood of detecting medium effects was over 95% but small effects under 50%
- The likelihood of detecting small effects was over 95% and medium effects under 50%
- The likelihood of detecting all effects was 100%, so no risk of type II error
- The likelihood of detecting all effects was 0%, so nothing could be concluded
E: The paper: "a power analysis was conducted to examine the potential for type II error. The likelihood of detecting medium effects was over 95 percent for an alpha level of .05 and the likelihood of detecting small effects was under 50 percent."

Q: What was the R² of use at T1 with direct effects only and with interactions (initial test)?
+ .37 (direct effects only) and .43 (with interactions)
- .07 (direct effects only) and .13 (with interactions)
- .77 (direct effects only) and .93 (with interactions)
- .17 (direct effects only) and .23 (with interactions)
E: Table 17(b): R²(PLS) T1: D ONLY .37; D + I .43.

Q: Which data did the cross-validation use?
+ Two new organizations: financial services (voluntary, N = 80) and retail electronics (mandatory, N = 53), total N = 133
- The original four organizations randomly split into two halves for cross-checking
- One university with 500 first-year students in a voluntary setting
- Two new organizations in the same industries as the original ones, N = 215
E: Table 18: Study 3: Financial Services, Research, 80, Voluntary; Study 4: Retail Electronics, Customer Service, 53, Mandatory; "N = 133 at each time period".

- The pattern was completely different and the model was rejected at the new organizations
- Variance explained was much lower, only about 10% at every time
- They could not be compared because the two data sets used entirely different item sets
E: The paper: "The results were consistent with studies 1 and 2... The pattern of results in this validation (Tables 21(a) and 21(b)) mirrors what was found in the preliminary test (Table 17)... The variance explained was quite comparable to that found in the preliminary test of UTAUT." (Table 21(a): pooled D+I adj. R² = .70).

Q: What share of the variance (adjusted) in usage intention does the paper conclude UTAUT accounts for?
+ About 70%
- About 7%
- About 17%
- About 100%
E: The paper: "UTAUT was able to account for 70 percent of the variance (adjusted R²) in usage intention—a substantial improvement over any of the original eight models and their extensions."

Q: Into how many main effects and moderators did UTAUT integrate the eight models' effects?
+ From 32 main effects and four moderators into four main effects and four moderators
- From 8 main effects and two moderators into 32 main effects and eight moderators
- From 4 main effects and four moderators into 32 main effects and no moderators
- From 100 main effects and four moderators into one main effect and no moderators
E: The paper: "UTAUT was successful in integrating key elements from among the initial set of 32 main effects and four moderators as determinants of intention and behavior collectively posited by eight alternate models into a model that incorporated four main effects and four moderators."

Q: What do the authors suggest about explaining 70% of the variance?
+ We may be approaching the practical limits of our ability to explain acceptance and usage decisions in organizations
- It is only a starting point; later studies will surely explain 100% of the variance
- It is too low and shows UTAUT fails to explain technology acceptance
- It is meaningless because intention variance can never be explained by a model
E: The paper: "Given that UTAUT explains as much as 70 percent of the variance in intention, it is possible that we may be approaching the practical limits of our ability to explain individual acceptance and usage decisions in organizations."

## Venkatesh 2003: limits and interpretation
Q: What limit about UTAUT's scales do the authors name?
+ Each construct used only the highest-loading items, which may drop facets and threaten content validity; the scales are preliminary
- Each construct used all items from the eight models, so the questionnaire was too long and people quit
- The scales are complete and need no further development or validation
- The scales had one item per construct, so internal consistency could not be computed
E: The paper: "one danger of this approach is that facets of each construct can be eliminated, thus threatening content validity... the measures for UTAUT should be viewed as preliminary and future research should be targeted at more fully developing and validating appropriate scales for each of the constructs with an emphasis on content validity".

Q: Which example shows items of one model missing from a core construct due to choosing highest-loading items?
+ MPCU items were not represented in performance expectancy
- TRA items were not represented in social influence
- SCT items were not represented in effort expectancy
- IDT items were not represented in facilitating conditions
E: The paper: "we found that choosing the highest-loading items resulted in items from some of the models not being represented in some of the core constructs (e.g., items from MPCU were not represented in performance expectancy)."

Q: What does the paper say about linking technology acceptance to individual or organizational outcomes?
+ Little research addresses it; it is often assumed that use leads to positive outcomes, but this remains untested
- The link has been tested many times, showing use always raises productivity
- The link needs no testing because use certainly leads to positive outcomes
- The link was studied in this paper with data on employees' productivity
E: The paper: "little to no research has addressed the link between user acceptance and individual or organizational usage outcomes. Thus, while it is often assumed that usage will result in positive outcomes, this remains to be tested."

Q: Which additional constructs does the paper suggest future research consider?
+ Behavioral expectation and habit
- Price and brand
- Color and size
- Speed and capacity
E: The paper: "future research should investigate other potential constructs such as behavioral expectation (Warshaw and Davis 1985) or habit (Venkatesh et al. 2000) in the nomological network."

Q: How many cells need adequate representation for a future study to test the moderators' full generalizability?
+ 24 cells (2 levels of voluntariness, 3 of experience, 2 of gender, at least 2 of age)
- 4 cells (one per moderator)
- 8 cells (one per original model)
- 100 cells (one per year of age)
E: The paper: "a research study that examines the generalizability of these findings with significant representation in each cell (total number of cells: 24; two levels of voluntariness, three levels of experience [no, limited, more], two levels of gender, and at least two levels of age [young vs. old])".

Q: Why should "UTAUT explains 70%" not be read as predicting students' learning?
+ It is the adjusted variance of intention to use, including moderators and interactions, among employees using business systems, unrelated to learning
- Because 70% is the variance of intention in university students and already a learning result
- Because UTAUT was built for education and 70% is the share of students passing courses
- Because 70% is the share of employees who quit after new technology within six months
E: UTAUT was tested on employees in organizations using business systems; main effects alone explain only about .27 to .30 adj. R² in pooled data; the paper has no learning content.

Q: Why might social influence in a classroom differ from the paper's results (design inference)?
+ In the paper it was mainly significant in mandatory settings; AI use in class can be voluntary or mandatory by design, so perceived voluntariness should be measured
- In classrooms social influence never exists because students do not care about others' opinions
- In classrooms social influence is always equally strong whether use is voluntary or mandatory
- In classrooms social influence comes only from school administrators and never from instructors
E: In the paper study 1 (voluntary) showed no significance; study 2 (mandatory) did; voluntariness was measured as a manipulation check (1 to 7 scale). Applying this to classrooms is design inference.

Q: Why is UTAUT alone not enough for a thesis on responsible AI (design inference)?
+ UTAUT explains intention and use, not whether use is appropriate or helps real learning; appropriate reliance (Module 10) and learning outcomes must also be measured
- Because UTAUT works only for technologies that appeared before 1990 and not for new technology
- Because UTAUT has proven that high acceptance always leads to better learning, so learning need not be measured
- Because UTAUT has only four variables, so it cannot be used in studies with more than one participant group
E: The paper itself notes no link between acceptance and outcomes; linking to Module 10 (appropriate reliance) is design inference.
