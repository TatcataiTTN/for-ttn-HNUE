Self-study: theoretical foundations for the thesis

Design and evaluate a responsible-AI model for an introductory programming course so that students genuinely learn and do not become dependent on AI. The site has 14 modules; each reads one keyword closely through its classic papers, then links back to the research questions.

Your thesis, restated

The problem

  • Schools react defensively to AI: offline exams, bans.
  • Graduates will use AI daily anyway, so they need to use it effectively and responsibly.
  • Information keeps getting cheaper and AI slop keeps growing; students need domain foundations to tell good from bad.
  • Active learning is disappearing when AI does the work; students must learn to "let go" of AI gradually.

Treatment model

  1. Write pseudocode and predict the output before running anything.
  2. Ask AI in a Socratic way: guiding questions, hints, explanations, not finished code.
  3. Students write a reflection and an explanation (how the exercise works, an A→B→C example).
  4. Measure academic performance and psychological variables.

Research design and four questions

Design: quasi-experiment (pretest + posttest, existing classes) or RCT (random recruitment); treatment group uses the model, control group uses AI freely; 4 weeks → 8 weeks → a full semester; at least one 4-week pilot.
RQ1. Compared with control, does the treatment improve programming ability without AI? → Modules 01, 02, 07, 13, 14
RQ2. How does the treatment affect psychological variables: AI dependence, metacognition, confidence, self-efficacy? → Modules 04, 05, 06, 08, 10
RQ3. How do students interact with the AI model (self-report, comparing strategies inside the treatment group)? → Modules 03, 09, 10, 11, 12
RQ4. What barriers do students and instructors meet when running it? → Modules 11, 12, 14

14 modules

Read in order if you are new. Each module has slides, detailed explanations, worked calculations, self-graded exercises, a quiz, and a "Link to the thesis" section.

How to study a module

  1. Watch the slides (← → keys, fullscreen available) to get the map.
  2. Read the details: concepts, formulas, background, real figures from the source paper.
  3. Do the self-graded exercises and try the interactive calculators.
  4. Take the quiz at the bottom and read the explanations.
  5. Read "Link to the thesis" and write down one design decision for your study.
