1. The concept

Bandura (1977) proposes a unifying theoretical framework to explain and predict psychological changes achieved by different modes of treatment. The thesis: psychological procedures, whatever their form, alter the level and strength of self-efficacy. Expectations of personal efficacy determine (a) whether coping behavior is initiated, (b) how much effort is expended, (c) how long it is sustained in the face of obstacles and aversive experiences.

Two different expectations. An outcome expectancy is a person's estimate that a given behavior will lead to certain outcomes. An efficacy expectation is the conviction that one can successfully execute the behavior required to produce the outcomes. They differ because a person can believe a course of action will produce outcomes, but if they seriously doubt they can perform the necessary activities, that information does not influence their behavior.

Three dimensions of efficacy expectations

DimensionMeaning
MagnitudeWhen tasks are ordered by difficulty, one person's expectations may be limited to simple tasks, another's extend to moderately difficult ones, another's include even the most taxing.
GeneralitySome experiences create circumscribed mastery expectations; others instill a generalized sense of efficacy well beyond the specific treatment situation.
StrengthWeak expectations are easily extinguished by disconfirming experiences; strong expectations persist despite them.

Three more points. Expectation is not the sole determinant: without component capabilities, expectation does not produce performance, and there are many things people can do with certainty that they do not do for lack of incentive. Effect on choice: people avoid threatening situations they believe exceed their coping skills and get involved when they judge themselves capable. Reciprocal effects: expectations influence performance and are altered by the cumulative effects of one's efforts, so both should be assessed at significant junctures.

Global outcome measures are of little use. In most studies expectations are assessed globally at a single point (participants are asked how much they expect to benefit). Such measures mix hope, wishful thinking, belief in the potency of the procedure and faith in the therapist; hence outcome expectations of this type have little relation to magnitude of behavioral change (Davison and Wilson 1973; Lick and Bootzin 1975).

Performance accomplishments: the typical method

Methods based on performance accomplishments convey efficacy information in more ways than mere evidence of improvement. In participant modeling, participants also acquire a generalizable coping skill. Therapists structure the environment so clients can perform successfully despite incapacities, using induction aids: preliminary modeling of threatening activities, graduated tasks, enactment over graduated temporal intervals, joint performance with the therapist, protective aids, and variation in the severity of the threat. As treatment progresses, the aids are withdrawn so clients cope unassisted, and self-directed mastery experiences are arranged to reinforce personal efficacy.

Comparative studies favor performance-based methods: performance desensitization produces substantially greater change than symbolic desensitization; real encounters are decidedly superior to imagined exposure; prolonged encounters are more effective than brief ones that end before successful performance.

Three effects of independent performance

  1. It creates additional exposure to former threats, giving further evidence of no longer being aversively aroused.
  2. It provides opportunities to perfect coping skills, lessening vulnerability to stress.
  3. If well executed, it produces success experiences that further reinforce expectations of competence.

Vicarious experience: variables that raise effectiveness

  • Seeing models overcome difficulties by determined effort works better than seeing adept models perform facilely.
  • Similarity to the model increases personal relevance.
  • Modeled behavior with clear outcomes conveys more efficacy information than ambiguous outcomes.
  • Diversified modeling (many different models succeed) beats a single model.

Verbal persuasion and arousal

"Placebo" studies show that merely telling people what benefit to expect produces little change in refractory behavior; the verbal influence there mainly raised outcome expectations, not self-efficacy. Bandura concludes social persuasion alone has limits for creating enduring self-efficacy but can contribute to success achieved through aided performance (people persuaded and given provisional aids mobilize more effort than those given only the aids). Raising expectations of competence by persuasion without arranging conditions for effective performance will most likely lead to failures that discredit the persuaders and further undermine self-efficacy.

On arousal, "false feedback" (telling phobics they are no longer upset) and misattribution treatments give mostly negative results, and are of little help to the highly anxious. Reductions from deceptive feedback are apt to be short-lived because illusory assurances are not a reliable way of creating durable self-expectations.

3. Cognitive processing of efficacy information

A distinction must be drawn between information contained in environmental events and information as processed and transformed by the individual. The impact on efficacy expectations depends on how information is cognitively appraised, so even success experiences do not necessarily create strong, generalized expectations.

Factor that weakens the value of successExample in the paper
DiscriminationPeople may behave boldly where safeguards exist yet keep their self-doubts under less secure conditions; reducing fear of imagined threats does little for efficacy if they believe success in imagery does not portend success in reality.
AttributionSuccess enhances self-efficacy more if seen as resulting from skill than from luck or special external aids; the more extensive the situational aids, the greater the chance behavior is ascribed to external factors. Failure lowers self-efficacy more when attributed to ability.
Effort versus abilitySuccess with minimal effort fosters ability ascriptions and strong self-efficacy; analogous success with high effort connotes lesser ability and has a weaker effect.
Task difficultySucceeding at easy tasks gives no new information; mastering challenging tasks conveys salient evidence of competence.
Rate and pattern of attainmentsPeople who experience setbacks but detect relative progress raise efficacy more than those who succeed but see performance leveling off.
The most effective treatment structure (Bandura and colleagues 1975). Generalized, lasting change is best achieved by participant methods that first use powerful induction procedures to develop capabilities, then remove external aids to verify personal efficacy, and finally use self-directed mastery to strengthen and generalize expectations.

Information from persuasive and arousal sources is also appraised: the credibility, prestige, trustworthiness, expertise and assuredness of persuaders; and the meaning given to arousal (people who attribute arousal to personal inadequacies lower efficacy expectations more than those who attribute it to situational factors). Bandura states that the effects of credibility on self-efficacy "remain to be investigated".

4. Compared with other theories

TheoryHow it differs from self-efficacy
Effectance motivation (White 1959)An intrinsic drive developing slowly through transactions with the environment, inferred from exploratory behavior and hard to verify independently. Self-efficacy is defined and measured independently of performance, predicts choice, generality and persistence, and can be created by more diverse, expeditious and powerful means.
Locus of control (Rotter 1966)Concerned mainly with causal beliefs about action-outcome contingencies, not personal efficacy. Example: a child who fails to grasp arithmetic and expects grades to depend entirely on skill has every reason to be demoralized.
Learned helplessness (Maier and Seligman 1976)Focuses exclusively on response-outcome expectancies. Bandura distinguishes two sources of futility: lack of efficacy (needs competence and expectations of personal effectiveness) and expecting behavior to have no effect on an unresponsive environment (needs changed environmental contingencies).
Dual-process theory (Wolpe)Assumes anxiety activates defensive behavior and anxiety reduction reinforces it. Bandura cites much evidence against it; anxiety and defensive behavior are coeffects of expectations of injurious consequences, with no fixed causal link.

Bandura also stresses context specificity: efficacy expectations do not operate as dispositional determinants independent of context; speaking of self-efficacy in general terms is as uninformative as speaking of nonspecific approach behavior. For example, self-efficacy in public speaking differs by subject matter, format and audience.

5. Evidence: microanalysis

Participants privately designated, on a list of 18 tasks ranked by increasing threat, those they could perform, then rated strength on a 100-point probability scale (10-unit intervals). Measures were taken before treatment, after treatment but before the behavioral posttest, and after the posttest. The posttest used different snakes (a different boa and a corn snake of markedly different appearance but equivalent threat) to test generality.

DesignResult
Adult severe snake phobics, three conditions: participant modeling (direct mastery), modeling alone (observation only), no treatmentPerformance accomplishments produced higher, more generalized and stronger efficacy expectations than vicarious experience, which exceeded the control condition. Behavior change corresponded closely to expectation change.
Correlation between efficacy level at end of treatment and approach behaviorr = .83 (enactive) and r = .84 (vicarious).
Task-level congruence microanalysis89% (enactive) and 86% (vicarious); only tasks never performed in the pretest: 82% and 79%.
Among those who all performed maximally in treatmentPredicting from past performance erred 28% (similar threat) and 52% (dissimilar); predicting from maximal efficacy expectations erred 21% and 24%.
Modeling alone: brief versus repeated observationBrief: level +9%, strength +5%, behavior +10%. Repeated successful observation: level +44%, strength +38%, behavior +35%.
Systematic desensitization (anxiety to imagined scenes fully extinguished)Participants emerged with widely differing efficacy expectations; behavior tracked them: r = .74; accurate on 85% of all tasks and 83% of tasks not performable in the pretest.
During treatment (task blocks)Efficacy predicted performance on 92% of all assessment tasks; on highly threatening tasks never done before: 84%.
Why efficacy predicts better than past performance. Bandura argues efficacy expectations are not merely cues from past behavior. They predicted well in vicarious and emotive treatments where participants engaged in no overt behavior, predicted behavior toward unfamiliar threats better than past performance, and predicted performance even on tasks never done. He also considers an alternative ("a superordinate mediator controlling both expectations and behavior") and argues such a mediator would have to be "exceedingly complex".

Limits acknowledged in the paper. More tests are needed for expectations from verbal persuasion and other arousal-reducing treatments; intensity and duration of effort on arduous or insoluble tasks should be studied by level and strength of expectation; cognitive processing needs more research. The quantitative evidence in the paper comes from adult snake-phobia experiments.

6. Compute it yourself

Exercise 1. The correlation between self-efficacy and behavior in the enactive group is r = .83. Compute r² (shared variance).
0.83² = 0.6889, about 69%.
Exercise 2. For the dissimilar threat, predicting from past performance erred 52% and from maximal efficacy 24%. By how many percentage points is efficacy more accurate?
52 − 24 = 28 percentage points.
Exercise 3. Repeated successful observation raised behavior by 35%; brief exposure by only 10%. How many percentage points more did repeated observation add?
35 − 10 = 25 percentage points.
Exercise 4. Task-level congruence was 89% in the enactive group and 86% in the vicarious group. By how many percentage points was the enactive group higher?
89 − 86 = 3 percentage points.

7. Interpretation traps

Trap 1: confusing efficacy expectation with outcome expectation. Believing "this approach will produce results" differs from believing "I can carry out this approach".
Trap 2: assuming self-efficacy alone gives performance. Bandura says expectation does not produce performance if component capabilities are lacking, and people also need incentives.
Trap 3: measuring self-efficacy globally. The paper stresses task-by-task microanalysis by difficulty, not global indices.
Trap 4: assuming success always raises self-efficacy. Success can be discounted if credited to luck, outside aid, an easy task, or if it needed great effort.
Trap 5: generalizing the evidence too far. The quantitative data are about snake phobia in adults; applying them to learning to program is inference needing testing (Module 13).

8. Linking to the thesis

AI and the "performance accomplishments" sourceIf AI writes most of the code, students may lack the very source Bandura considers most influential. If students credit success to the AI (external aid), the cognitive-processing section predicts smaller gains in self-efficacy. This is a design hypothesis, not an empirical result.
A fading structureBandura's sequence (powerful induction, remove aids, self-directed mastery) is close to a treatment group design: AI models and Socratically hints, is faded, then students work independently without AI (link to Module 03).
RQ2, measurementMeasure programming self-efficacy per task ordered by difficulty (for example: write a loop, handle an array, debug), with a 0 to 100 strength rating as Bandura did; before and after; and compare with no-AI scores to compute congruence. Link to Module 05: self-efficacy that matches real scores is a form of calibration.
